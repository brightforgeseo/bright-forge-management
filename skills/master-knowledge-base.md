## 2. Core Philosophy and Operating Principles

### Sustainable, Long-Term Growth

Every recommendation, every link, every piece of content has to make sense in five years. Quick-fix tactics are explicitly off the table. PBNs, link farms, keyword stuffing, cloaking, scraped content, doorway pages: never. Not because they don't work in the short term but because they create existential risk for clients.

### Data-Driven, Never Vibes-Based

Every recommendation is backed by analysis: GA4, Search Console, ranking data, competitor research, log files, crawl data. "Best practices" is not a justification; data is. When data is genuinely unavailable, the answer is "we don't have visibility on that" not made-up confidence.

### Client-Centric

SEO exists to drive business outcomes, not vanity rankings. Before any keyword strategy, the question is: what does this client actually sell, to whom, at what margin? A keyword that ranks but doesn't convert is failure, not success.

### Transparent Communication

Set realistic expectations. SEO takes 3-6 months to show meaningful movement, often longer in competitive industries. Anyone promising faster is lying. When something goes wrong, the client hears about it from us first, with context and a recovery plan, never as a surprise in their own analytics.

### White-Hat Only

Non-negotiable. Every technique used must be defensible in a public audit. If we wouldn't be comfortable explaining the tactic on a podcast, we don't do it.

### The "Would a Senior SEO Call This Shite?" Test

Every deliverable, every audit, every report goes through this filter before it leaves the building. It catches:

- Generic best-practice rehashes with no specific insight
- Data dumps without interpretation
- Recommendations that don't survive five seconds of scrutiny
- Numbered phases that exist for the appearance of structure rather than to communicate
- Meta-commentary about the audit instead of the audit itself
- AI-flavoured prose that nobody would actually write

If the answer is yes, the work doesn't go out.

## 7. Article Writing and Optimisation Standards

This is the operational heart of content delivery. These standards are non-negotiable.

### Critical Rules (Always Follow)

**Keyword integration.**

- Primary keyword (from article title): 3-5 times in body. Count body only, metadata is separate.
- Secondary keywords: 2-4 times each in body
- No keyword repeats across articles in the same cluster
- Distribute keywords across intro, body, H3s, conclusion
- Metadata (title tag, meta description) gets 1-2 keyword uses, separate from body count

**Linking standards.**

- 2-5 internal links per article depending on length
- Maximum 1 link per service URL per article (no duplication)
- Homepage exception: can be linked 1-3 times if natural
- No repeated anchor text across articles in the same batch
- No repeated link positions across articles in the same batch

**Article output format.**

- Always markdown
- Embedded links: `[anchor text](URL)`
- Headings: `#` `##` `###` hierarchy
- SEO metadata block at bottom (meta title, meta description, focus keyword, secondary keywords)
- Ready to paste into WordPress or any CMS

**British/Australian English by default** unless client is US-based. Common mistakes to avoid: "optimization" (wrong) vs "optimisation" (right) for AU/UK clients. Check client locale before writing.

### Forbidden Stylistic Choices

**No em dashes. Ever.** Use commas, full stops, or semicolons instead. This applies to every piece of output, not just articles. Em dashes are the AI tell that triggers "this was written by ChatGPT" reactions.

**No AI-flavoured prose.** Avoid "in today's fast-paced world," "navigating the complexities of," "in the realm of," "delve into," "it's important to note that," "when it comes to," "at the end of the day." If a phrase shows up in every other AI-generated article, it doesn't appear here.

**No filler intros.** The first 50 words must do work. No throat-clearing, no "have you ever wondered," no rhetorical setup paragraphs. Lead with the answer or the hook.

**No bullet-point dependence.** Prose first. Lists only when the content is genuinely list-shaped (steps, components, criteria). Lists for the sake of scannability are weak writing.

**No hedging.** "May," "might," "could potentially," "in some cases" weaken every sentence they appear in. State the thing.

### Article Structure (Standard Blog Article)

**Introduction (100-150 words).**
- Hook in first sentence (problem, surprising fact, direct statement)
- Establish stakes or context
- Indicate what the article will deliver
- Primary keyword integrated once, naturally

**Body (800-1500 words for standard article, 2000+ for pillar).**
- 3-6 main sections under H2s
- Sub-points under H3s where structure demands it
- Each section delivers a specific point or answers a specific question
- Evidence: stats, examples, screenshots, expert quotes, research citations
- Smooth transitions between sections
- Primary keyword used 2-3 more times across body
- Secondary keywords integrated naturally

**Conclusion (100-150 words).**
- Summarise key takeaways concisely
- Practical next step or CTA
- Optional: one final primary keyword use

### Content Quality Markers

**Readability.** Flesch Reading Ease 60-70 typically. Sentence variety: short, medium, long. Paragraph breaks every 3-4 sentences. Subheadings every 200-300 words.

**Evidence density.** Every claim either obvious, sourced, or backed by example. Vague claims ("studies show," "many businesses") are weak; specific evidence ("a 2024 study by [source]" or a real example) is strong.

**Voice.** Active voice unless passive serves a specific purpose. Direct address to the reader where appropriate ("you," "your business"). Confident assertion, not tentative suggestion.

**Specificity.** Generic advice ("use long-tail keywords") is weak. Specific advice ("target 4-7 word phrases with monthly volume between 100-1000 and keyword difficulty under 30") is strong.

### SEO Metadata Standards

**Meta title.**
- 50-60 characters
- Primary keyword toward front
- Brand name at end (optional, depending on client preference)
- Compelling, not just descriptive
- No clickbait

**Meta description.**
- 150-160 characters
- Primary keyword once
- Clear value proposition
- Soft CTA where natural
- Written for clicks, not just for length

**URL slug.**
- 3-5 words ideal
- Hyphens between words
- Primary keyword included
- No stop words (a, the, of, in) unless removing changes meaning
- Lowercase

### Article Quality Checklist

Before any article is delivered:

- [ ] Primary keyword in title (H1)
- [ ] Primary keyword in body 3-5 times
- [ ] Secondary keywords each present 2-4 times
- [ ] No duplicate service URLs
- [ ] No repeated anchor text from previous batch articles
- [ ] No repeated link positions from previous batch articles
- [ ] 2-5 internal links present
- [ ] 1-2 external authoritative links (where appropriate)
- [ ] Meta title 50-60 characters
- [ ] Meta description 150-160 characters
- [ ] URL slug clean and keyword-relevant
- [ ] Header hierarchy correct (one H1, logical H2/H3 structure)
- [ ] Image alt text present on all images
- [ ] No em dashes anywhere
- [ ] British/Australian English (unless US client)
- [ ] No AI-tell phrases
- [ ] Readability Flesch 60-70
- [ ] Grammar and spelling clean
- [ ] CTA present and appropriate
- [ ] Mobile formatting verified (no awkward line breaks)

### Content Refresh Strategy

**Triggers for refresh:**
- Page ranking positions 4-10 (close to better positions)
- Page ranking positions 11-20 (close to page 1)
- Traffic decline >30% over 6 months
- CTR decline >20% over 3 months
- Outdated stats, dates, or claims
- Thin content (under 500 words on a topic that warrants more)

**Refresh approach:**
- Add 200-500 words of new, substantive content
- Update statistics, dates, examples
- Improve keyword integration if weak
- Add new internal links to recent relevant content
- Enhance with images, examples, or interactive elements
- Update meta description for better CTR
- Update "last updated" date prominently

### Cluster Article Production Rules

When producing a batch of articles for the same client (typical Bright Forge workflow):

- Maintain a master keyword tracker so primary keywords don't repeat across the cluster
- Maintain a link distribution tracker to vary anchor text and link positions
- Each article should advance the cluster's overall topical authority, not duplicate it
- Cross-link related cluster articles where genuinely relevant (not forced)
- Keep tone, voice, and structure consistent across the cluster

## 8. Interlinking Strategy and Variation Framework

Internal linking is where most SEO content fails. Patterns repeat. Anchor text duplicates. Service URLs get hammered with 4 links per article. This section is the framework for not doing that.

### Core Principles

**One link per service URL per article.** Maximum. Exception is the homepage, which can take 1-3 links per article if natural. Every other service URL gets one shot.

**Vary across batches.** If yesterday's article opened with a homepage link in paragraph 2 and closed with a CTA to the contact page, today's article does something different. Link positions, anchor text, density, and target page distribution all rotate.

**Relevance trumps optimisation.** A link forced into an article to hit a quota is a bad link. If a section doesn't naturally call for a link, don't add one. Better to deliver a 3-link article that flows than a 5-link article that reads like a sales sheet.

### Link Position Patterns

Rotate these across batches:

**Pattern A: Early CTA**
- Homepage link in intro (paragraph 1-2)
- Service links scattered through body
- Final CTA in conclusion

**Pattern B: Progressive Build**
- Service link in section 2
- Homepage link mid-article
- Service link near end
- CTA in conclusion

**Pattern C: Distributed Authority**
- Service link early (section 1)
- Homepage link middle
- Service link section 4-5
- Light CTA close

**Pattern D: Late Conversion**
- No links in intro or section 1
- All linking concentrated in second half
- Strong CTA in conclusion

**Pattern E: Sidebar Approach**
- Single homepage link
- Multiple cross-links to other blog content
- Final CTA in conclusion

Track which pattern was used for the most recent 3-5 articles per client to ensure rotation.

### Anchor Text Variation

For service pages, rotate between:

- **Exact match:** the literal service keyword ("car auctions")
- **Partial match:** keyword variation ("browse our auctions")
- **Branded:** brand + service ("RAW2K car auctions")
- **Action-oriented:** verb-led ("explore available vehicles")
- **Descriptive:** outcome or benefit ("find your next vehicle")

For homepage links, rotate between:

- **Brand mention:** just the brand name ("RAW2K")
- **Natural reference:** "our platform," "our team," "our service"
- **Action-oriented:** "get in touch," "start searching," "see our work"
- **Descriptive:** "the UK's leading [category]"

Never use "all cars" as anchor text for RAW2K. Never use "click here" for any client; it's lazy and wastes the SEO value.

### Link Density by Article Length

**500-800 words:** 2 links maximum (typically 1 homepage + 1 service)
**800-1200 words:** 3 links standard (1-2 homepage + 1-2 service)
**1200-1500 words:** 4 links standard (2 homepage + 2 service, or distribution variant)
**1500+ words:** 4-5 links maximum (only if natural; not forced)

### Client-Specific Linking Notes

**RAW2K**
- Registration URL: https://www.raw2k.co.uk/register (use exact URL)
- Main CTA: [car auctions](https://www.raw2k.co.uk/car-auctions/)
- Never "all cars" as anchor text

(Add other client-specific linking rules as they're established. This list expands per client onboarding.)

### Cross-Link Strategy (Blog-to-Blog)

In addition to service page links, articles should cross-link to other relevant articles. This builds topical authority and improves crawl distribution.

- 1-2 cross-links to other blog articles per piece
- Link to articles on closely related topics, not random pieces
- Use the article's actual title or a near-paraphrase as anchor text
- Link from the section that thematically aligns with the linked piece

## 9. Keyword Research and Content Strategy

### Discovery Phase

**Understanding the client business comes first.**

Before any keyword tool is opened, understand:
- What the client actually sells, in their own language
- Who their customers are and how those customers describe their problem
- Margin and profitability across product/service lines (focus SEO budget on profitable services)
- Sales cycle length and complexity
- Geographic constraints (local, national, international)
- Seasonal patterns

**Then expand into keyword research.**

- Industry terminology mapping (insider terms vs customer terms)
- Competitor keyword analysis (Ahrefs, SEMrush, SE Ranking)
- Search Console mining for existing low-impression queries
- "People Also Ask" expansion via AlsoAsked
- Reddit, Quora, and industry forums for emerging questions
- Customer service inbox (gold mine for real customer language)

### Keyword Evaluation

For every candidate keyword, assess:

**Search volume.** What's the realistic monthly search volume? Tools disagree; cross-reference at least two.

**Keyword difficulty.** Combination of competition strength and content depth required to rank. Use the manual SERP analysis below, not just tool scores.

**Search intent.** Informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational. Match content type to intent or the page won't convert no matter how it ranks.

**Commercial value.** What's the customer worth at this query? A low-volume keyword that converts at 10% with a $5,000 average customer value beats a high-volume keyword that converts at 0.2% with a $50 customer.

**Relevance.** Does ranking for this actually help the business? "SEO tips" might have volume but it doesn't drive Bright Forge's business; "white label SEO Australia" does.

**Time to rank.** Realistic estimate of months to position 1-3 given current site authority and content gap. New sites won't rank for competitive head terms in 6 months; that's a fact, not a defeatist attitude.

### Manual SERP Analysis (Beyond Tool Scores)

For every priority keyword:

1. Search the keyword in an incognito window with appropriate locale
2. Count high-authority domains on page 1 (DR 50+)
3. Analyse content depth: word counts, multimedia, structure
4. Assess intent match of ranking content
5. Identify SERP features (featured snippet, PAA, video pack, local pack)
6. Note commercial intent: are top results selling something, informing, or both?

**Difficulty calibration:**
- Easy: <3 high-authority domains, thin content ranking, clear gaps
- Medium: 3-5 high-authority domains, moderate quality, gaps available
- Hard: 6+ high-authority domains, comprehensive content, no obvious gaps
- Very Hard: page 1 dominated by major brands, exhaustive content, strong technical baseline

### Search Intent Classification

**Informational intent signals:**
- "how to," "what is," "guide to," "tips for"
- "learn," "tutorial," "explained"
- Question words: who, what, where, when, why, how
- "best practices," "checklist," "examples"

**Commercial investigation signals:**
- "best," "top," "review," "comparison"
- "vs" or "versus"
- "alternative to," "like [brand]"
- "[year]" indicating shopping for current options

**Transactional signals:**
- "buy," "price," "cost," "cheap," "discount"
- "deal," "coupon," "free shipping"
- "near me," "open now"
- Branded product names with modifiers

**Navigational signals:**
- Brand names
- Login terms
- Specific product/service names
- Company + city/location

### Keyword Clustering

Group keywords by:

- **Topic** (semantic similarity)
- **Intent** (informational/commercial/transactional)
- **Funnel stage** (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU)
- **Page type** (blog post, service page, location page, comparison page)

Each cluster maps to a content piece or page. Avoid creating multiple pages targeting the same intent and topic; that's keyword cannibalisation, not coverage.

### Topic Cluster Strategy

**Pillar content (high-volume, broad terms):**
- 2,000-5,000 words
- Comprehensive coverage of a topic
- Links out to all cluster content
- Updated quarterly
- Targets: main keyword + 5-10 variations

**Cluster content (specific subtopics):**
- 1,000-2,000 words
- Detailed coverage of a specific angle
- Links back to pillar
- Cross-links to related cluster pieces
- Targets: long-tail variations

**Supporting content (very specific):**
- 500-1,000 words
- Answers a specific narrow question
- Links to relevant cluster content
- Targets: very long-tail, often question-based

### Content Calendar Planning

**Monthly output by retainer:**
- Standard (16 hours): 2-4 articles/month
- Growth (24 hours): 4-6 articles/month
- Performance (32 hours): 6-8 articles/month

**Content mix:**
- 40% informational (TOFU): top-of-funnel, broad audience
- 35% consideration (MOFU): comparison, buying guides
- 25% commercial (BOFU): service-specific, conversion-focused

This is a default mix. For conversion-focused clients with mature TOFU, shift toward MOFU/BOFU. For new sites without authority, more TOFU early to build topical authority and indexation.

### Keyword Opportunity Scoring

For prioritising which keywords get content first:

```
Priority Score = (Volume × Relevance × Conversion Potential) / (Difficulty × Time to Rank)

Volume: 1-10 normalised for industry
Relevance: 1-10 fit to business
Conversion Potential: 1-10 commercial intent
Difficulty: 1-10 competition
Time to Rank: 1-10 (estimated months / 2)
```

Scoring bands:
- 15+: high priority, build now
- 10-15: medium priority, build in next quarter
- <10: low priority, queue or skip

## 10. Technical SEO Audits

### Audit Standards

Bright Forge audits are written as prose, not as tables or numbered phases. They interpret findings, not just list them. They're written so a senior SEO would read them and respect the work.

**Audit anti-patterns to avoid:**
- Numbered "phases" that exist for the appearance of structure
- Tables where prose would communicate better
- Generic best-practice rehashes ("you should have a sitemap")
- Recommendations without specifics ("improve site speed")
- Meta-commentary about the audit ("this section will cover...")
- AI-flavoured prose with no hands-on insight

**Audit positives:**
- Contractions used naturally (2:1 ratio with formal phrasing)
- Specific issues with specific page examples
- Concrete fix instructions, not vague directives
- Month 1 work integrates content and technical together (not artificial phases)
- AI visibility framed as an outcome of good SEO, not a separate workstream
- Testimonial credibility checked
- Stock image ratios noted
- AI-generated design flagged where present
- Data interpreted, not just listed

### Audit Components

**1. Site architecture and crawlability**
- XML sitemap validation and coverage
- Robots.txt review (this matters; the RKMRS incident is the cautionary tale)
- Internal linking structure and equity distribution
- Orphaned page identification
- Crawl depth analysis (3-click rule for important pages)
- Pagination handling
- Faceted navigation handling (e-commerce)

**2. URL structure**
- Clean, descriptive URLs
- Proper hierarchy
- Canonical tag implementation
- Parameter handling
- Redirect chain identification (chains beyond 2 hops are problems)

**3. Performance and Core Web Vitals**
- LCP target: <2.5s
- INP target: <200ms
- CLS target: <0.1
- Server response time (TTFB)
- Image optimisation (WebP/AVIF, lazy loading, dimensions)
- Code minification
- Caching implementation
- CDN usage
- JavaScript impact

**4. On-page SEO elements**
- Title tags (50-60 characters, unique, keyword-aligned)
- Meta descriptions (150-160 characters, unique, compelling)
- Header tag hierarchy (one H1, logical H2/H3)
- Schema markup implementation and validation
- Open Graph and Twitter Card tags
- Image alt text coverage
- Content depth and quality signals

**5. Mobile optimisation**
- Mobile-friendly test pass
- Responsive design implementation
- Touch element sizing
- Viewport configuration
- Mobile page speed
- Mobile-first indexing readiness

**6. Indexation status**
- Pages indexed vs submitted
- Index bloat identification (thin pages, duplicates, parameter pages)
- Duplicate content detection
- Noindex audit
- Canonical errors

**7. Security and HTTPS**
- SSL certificate validity and expiry
- Mixed content issues
- HSTS implementation
- Secure resource loading

**8. JavaScript SEO (where applicable)**
- Critical content present in initial HTML
- Server-side rendering or static generation evaluation
- Meta tags accessible in source HTML
- Structured data in initial HTML
- Crawlable navigation (no JS-only nav for important pages)

**9. AI search visibility (GEO/AEO assessment)**
- Peec or similar tool for prompt-level visibility analysis
- Citation analysis: is the brand cited in AI overviews?
- Entity coverage assessment
- Structured content assessment for AI ingestion
- See section 12 for full AEO methodology

### Audit Issue Prioritisation

**Critical (fix within 1 week):**
- Server errors (5xx)
- Site architecture failures
- Security vulnerabilities
- Core Web Vitals failures on revenue pages
- Deindexation risks (accidental noindex, robots.txt blocks)
- ClickFix or other malware (handle as emergency)

**High (fix within 1 month):**
- Missing or weak meta descriptions on key pages
- Broken internal links
- Slow page speeds on key pages
- Mobile usability issues
- Duplicate content on indexed pages
- Schema markup errors

**Medium (fix within 2-3 months):**
- Optimisation opportunities (not failures)
- Content depth improvements
- Link equity redistribution
- Schema enhancements
- Image optimisation at scale

**Low (ongoing):**
- Minor performance gains
- Content refreshes
- Additional schema types
- Enhanced rich result formatting

### Standard Audit Format

The deliverable is a Word document or PDF, not a spreadsheet. Sections:

**Executive overview.** 2-3 paragraphs. Where the site is, what's working, what's broken, what the priorities are. Written so a non-technical client gets the gist.

**Technical foundation.** Architecture, crawlability, indexation. Prose-led. Specific page examples for issues. Concrete fixes.

**On-page and content quality.** Meta data, headers, content depth, internal linking. Interpret, don't list.

**Performance.** Core Web Vitals, speed, mobile. Specific failing pages, specific causes, specific fixes.

**Schema and structured data.** Current implementation, errors, opportunities.

**AI search visibility (where relevant).** Peec analysis, citation rate, entity coverage gaps, structured content assessment.

**Roadmap.** Month 1, Month 2, Month 3. Integrated content and technical work, not artificial phase separation. Specific deliverables with specific outcomes.

**Closing.** No corporate sign-off fluff. State the priority. Indicate next step.

### The RKMRS Lesson

In a previous engagement, an RKMRS site change resulted in robots.txt blocking that caused a 63% traffic decline before it was caught. Standing operating procedure since:

- Robots.txt is monitored daily on critical client sites
- Any robots.txt change is reviewed before deployment
- Indexation status is checked weekly
- Sudden traffic drops trigger immediate technical investigation, not "let's wait and see"

- Q&A management (questions answered, especially competitor-planted ones)
- Service description completeness
- Operating hours accuracy

Use the score for upsell conversations: a client at 60% GBP health has a clear path to 90%+ that justifies additional work.

### Evaluation Modes (Service Businesses)

For service businesses (legal, trades, healthcare, professional services), strategy splits by user intent mode:

**Emergency mode.**
- User has an urgent problem (broken pipe, legal threat, medical concern)
- Decision criteria: responsiveness, immediate availability, trust signals
- Content emphasis: phone-first CTAs, hours of operation, response time guarantees, "available now" signals
- Page design: minimal friction, large clickable phone numbers, clear emergency vs non-emergency paths

**Project mode.**
- User is researching a planned project (renovation, hiring, treatment course)
- Decision criteria: expertise, value, comprehensive understanding
- Content emphasis: portfolio, case studies, detailed methodology, pricing transparency, comparison resources
- Page design: information-rich, longer engagement, multiple consideration touchpoints

Both modes need separate content and conversion paths. A site optimised only for project mode misses emergency revenue; a site optimised only for emergency mode looks shallow to project users.

## 12. GEO and AEO

### Why This Matters

Search is shifting. AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini are increasingly the place where users find answers. Traditional ranking matters less when 30%+ of queries get answered without a click. Bright Forge positions deliberately ahead of this curve.

GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) and AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation) overlap heavily. The goal in both: be the source AI cites when answering questions in your domain.

### AI Search Visibility Audit

Standard methodology using Peec or similar:

1. Generate 50-100 prompts a target customer might ask
2. Run prompts across major AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Google AI Overviews)
3. Track citation rate: how often is the client's site cited?
4. Track competitor citations
5. Identify topics where client is invisible
6. Identify content formats that get cited (FAQ, listicle, expert quote, structured guide)

The RevFactor audit (Pro Closer) showed 0% citation across 75 prompts. That's the floor; it's recoverable but it identifies the work to do.

### What Gets Cited

AI engines cite content that is:

- **Structurally clean.** Clear H2/H3 hierarchy, FAQ schema, list and table formats where appropriate
- **Substantively answering specific questions.** Not "everything you need to know about X" but "how long does X take to recover from"
- **Authoritatively attributed.** Named author with credentials, organisation with clear identity
- **Cross-referenced elsewhere.** Cited in other authoritative sources, mentioned by name
- **Up to date.** Recent publication or update dates
- **Specific.** Numbers, examples, named cases, concrete claims (not vague generalities)

### Content Production for AEO

**Structural patterns that work:**

- Question-as-H2 followed by direct answer in first paragraph
- Definition-style opening on key terms
- "How to" lists with numbered steps
- Comparison tables for vs-style queries
- FAQ sections at the end of articles
- Quoted expert statements with attribution

**Schema markup for AEO:**
- FAQPage schema for genuine Q&A content
- HowTo schema for step-by-step guides
- Article schema with proper author markup
- Organization schema with detailed entity information
- Service schema with location and offering details

### Entity Authority Building

The web is increasingly understood by AI as a graph of entities (people, organisations, products, places) and their relationships. Building entity authority for clients:

- Complete and detailed Google Business Profile
- Wikipedia presence where the entity is notable
- Wikidata entry creation and maintenance
- Consistent NAP across web
- Industry directory listings
- Mentions on authoritative sources (with or without links)
- Author bios with verifiable credentials
- Brand mentions on news, podcast, and social platforms

### Position on AI Search

Three things to communicate to clients consistently:

1. AI search is real and growing fast; ignoring it is a strategic error
2. Good AI search visibility is downstream of good traditional SEO; the fundamentals don't change, the structure of content production shifts
3. AI search visibility is an outcome of good SEO, not a separate workstream. We don't have an "AEO package"; AEO is integrated into how we work.

## 17. Communication and Voice Standards

### Universal Voice Rules

**Direct and confident.** State the thing. Don't hedge unless genuinely uncertain.

**Technically credible.** Use technical terms accurately when they're the right tool. Don't over-explain to clients capable of understanding.

**Never patronising.** Treat clients as intelligent adults running businesses they understand better than we do.

**Specific over generic.** Names, numbers, examples beat generalities.

**Honest about limits.** "We don't know yet" is sometimes the right answer. "We can't promise that" is sometimes the right answer.

### Forbidden Phrases and Patterns

**The em dash.** Never. Use commas, full stops, or semicolons.

**AI-tell phrases.** "Navigate the complexities of," "in today's fast-paced world," "delve into," "the realm of," "it's important to note that," "when it comes to," "at the end of the day," "elevate your strategy," "unlock the potential."

**Vague hedging.** "May," "might," "could potentially," "in some cases" used to weaken every claim.

**Corporate softening.** "Going forward," "moving the needle," "bandwidth," "circle back," "touch base."

**Agency-speak that means nothing.** "Holistic approach," "synergy," "leverage," "best-in-class," "world-class," "cutting-edge."

### Email Communication Standards

**Subject lines.** Specific. "Q4 strategy meeting recap and action items" beats "Following up."

**Opening.** Don't start with "I hope this email finds you well." Get to the point. "Quick update on the Smith account schema work" beats throat-clearing.

**Body.** Short paragraphs. One topic per email where possible. If multiple topics, clear visual separation.

**Closing.** Clear ask or next step if there is one. "Let me know if you want me to push the schema deployment to Friday or hold for next week" beats "Let me know your thoughts."

### Slack and Quick Messaging

Even more direct. Casual is fine. Tag appropriately. Threads for sub-conversations. Avoid the "U up?" pattern (sending "Hi" then waiting before stating the request).

### Client Meeting Preparation

Before any client meeting:
- Review the report being discussed
- Anticipate likely questions
- Have supporting data ready
- Know specifically what's planned for the next month
- Be ready to adjust strategy based on client input
- Have a clear set of decisions or commitments needed

### Difficult Client Conversations

When traffic drops, when expectations don't match reality, when something has gone wrong:

- Lead with clarity, not defensiveness
- State what happened
- Explain why
- Outline recovery or correction
- Take responsibility where appropriate; don't take responsibility where it's not warranted
- Don't manufacture optimism; manufacture credibility

### Proofreading Standard

All Ben's writing gets proofread and polished. Fix spelling, grammar, suggest better word choices. Keep his voice. The polish should be invisible; the voice should be Ben's.

### Tone Adjustments by Client

- Direct/blunt clients: match the directness; don't over-formalise
- Technical clients: use technical terms freely
- Non-technical clients: don't use jargon without translation
- Formal industries (legal, finance): more measured tone
- Trades/construction: blunt is welcome, no fancy language
